Tuition fee receipt format (with free sample)

A tuition fee receipt is proof for the parent that a fee was paid, and proof for you of what was collected. Here is what to include, a sample you can copy, and how to number receipts so nothing gets lost.

What a fee receipt should include

FieldWhy it matters
Receipt numberA running number (R-0001, R-0002…) lets you find any payment later and stops duplicates.
Date of paymentSettles questions about late fees.
Student and parent nameShows who paid, especially when siblings study together.
Month or period the fee is forThe most common source of disputes: "Was that for October or November?"
Amount in numbers and wordsWords prevent editing of the figure.
Payment mode and referenceUPI reference or cheque number helps match your bank statement.
Balance due, if anyMakes part payments clear for both sides.
Your centre name, contact and signatureMakes the receipt identifiable and trustworthy.

Do small tuition centres need a GST receipt?

Generally, a tutor or centre whose total turnover is below the GST registration threshold is not registered for GST and issues a simple receipt like the one above, without a GSTIN or tax lines. If you are registered for GST, your receipt or invoice must follow GST invoice rules. Rules change, so confirm your own case with a chartered accountant.

Numbering receipts without a receipt book

Keep one payment log where every row is one payment. Give each row the next number in sequence and never reuse or skip a number. In a spreadsheet, a formula such as ="R-"&TEXT(ROW()-4,"0000") numbers each row automatically.
